Complaints and feedback policy

Last updated: November 2026

We want to hear from you, whether something went well or not. Raising a concern will never affect the care you, or someone you love, receive from us.

Who can give feedback

Anyone: people in our care, former clients, family members, referrers and visitors. You can also ask someone you trust to raise a concern on your behalf. If it is about a person in treatment, we may need their consent before we can discuss the details with you.

How to raise a concern

  • Talk to us. Many concerns can be sorted out quickly by speaking to any member of staff or the person in charge of the shift.
  • Call us at any time on +254 795 180 704.
  • Email restoracentreforrecovery@gmail.com, with “Complaint” or “Feedback” in the subject line.
  • In writing at the centre, Old Namanga Rd, Kitengela.

It helps to tell us what happened, when, who was involved, and what you would like us to do about it.

What happens next

  • We will acknowledge your complaint promptly and tell you who is looking into it.
  • A senior member of our team who was not involved will review what happened, and may ask to speak with you.
  • We will explain what we found, apologise where we got something wrong, and tell you what we are changing as a result.
  • Your complaint is handled confidentially and shared only with the people who need to look into it.

If you are not satisfied

If you are unhappy with our response, tell us and we will review it again. You can also contact the relevant regulator: the Kenya Medical Practitioners and Dentists Council about the conduct of a doctor, or the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner about how your personal information has been handled.

Urgent safety concerns

If someone is in immediate danger, call 999 or 112 first, then tell us. See our emergency notice.

Compliments and suggestions

We are always glad to hear what helped, and ideas for doing better. Your words may be shared with the team, never with your name unless you say so.

Restoring lives rebuilding futures

In an emergency or if someone has overdosed, call 999 or 112 straight away.
