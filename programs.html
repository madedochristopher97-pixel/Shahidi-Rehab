Treatment, one stage at a time.

Your assessment decides where you start. Here is what each stage involves. We are open 24 hours a day.

The 90-day program

Residential treatment runs for 90 days, in three phases. Your assessment decides where you begin.

Days 1 to 14

Medical stabilisation and detox

Supervised withdrawal with nursing care around the clock, so the physical risks are managed safely.

Days 15 to 60

The core therapeutic phase

Individual therapy, group therapy, trauma processing, psychiatric review where it is needed, and structured family work.

Days 61 to 90

Integration and relapse prevention

Preparing for life after the centre, including aftercare planning with the household you are returning to.

Why 90 days?

Clinical research shows that 90 days is the minimum length of stay associated with measurable improvement in long-term outcomes for substance use disorders. Programs shorter than 30 days largely do not change long-term outcomes, so be cautious of any rehab quoting strong results on a 28-day stay.

When addiction is not the only struggle

Many people who struggle with alcohol or drugs are also living with depression, anxiety, ADHD or a mood disorder. Sometimes the mental health problem came first and substances became a way of coping. Sometimes drinking or drug use caused the problem, or made it worse. Either way, treating only one of them usually leads to relapse in the other. Clinicians call this a dual diagnosis, or co-occurring disorders.

Why it matters

Recovery is far more likely to last when the addiction and the mental health condition are treated together, in one plan, by the same team. That is how we work.

Substances can mimic symptoms

Intoxication and withdrawal can themselves cause low mood, anxiety, poor concentration and agitation. Clinicians are required to rule this out, so getting a full picture sometimes needs a period without alcohol or drugs first.

Common questions

How long is the residential program?

The program runs for 90 days. Research shows 90 days is the minimum length of stay linked to measurable improvement in long-term outcomes. Programs shorter than 30 days largely do not change long-term outcomes, so be cautious of any rehab quoting strong results on a 28-day stay.

Will detox be painful?

Safety and comfort come first. Our medical team uses medication to ease symptoms such as tremors, nausea, anxiety and poor sleep.

Can my family visit?

Yes, on scheduled visiting days. Visits are an important part of the family program. Some restrictions apply during the early days of detox and stabilisation. The clinical team explains the visiting schedule on admission and updates it as treatment progresses.

Will my employer find out?

No. We never share your admission or medical records with employers, insurers or anyone else without your written consent.

How many sessions will I have?

Most clients attend 2 to 5 sessions a day, including therapy, group discussions and psychoeducation. Over a month that adds up to roughly 60 to 100 sessions, shaped around your treatment plan.

Can you guarantee I will not relapse after discharge?

No, and you should be wary of anyone who does. What we can do is significantly reduce the risk, through evidence-based treatment, relapse prevention strategies and continued follow-up care. Recovery is a lifelong journey and maintaining it takes ongoing effort, support and changes to the way you live.

What if I want to leave early?

Voluntary clients can leave at any time, but we strongly discourage it. Most requests to leave early are a symptom of the emotional turbulence of early recovery rather than a settled wish to stop treatment. The clinical team will work to address what is driving the request, and your family is involved in any conversation about early departure.

Can I speak to someone who has been through the program?

Yes, with consent on both sides. We can introduce you to a former client or family member willing to talk about their experience. We do not offer this publicly, because we treat former clients’ privacy as paramount, but during a serious admissions conversation we can often arrange it.

Not sure which stage fits?

Tell us what is happening and we will recommend a starting point. There is no pressure and no obligation.
