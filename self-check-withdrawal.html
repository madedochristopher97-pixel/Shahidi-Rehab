CIWA-Ar: measuring alcohol withdrawal safely

Stopping alcohol suddenly after weeks or months of heavy drinking can be dangerous. The Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment for Alcohol, revised (CIWA-Ar), published by Sullivan and colleagues in 1989, is the standard tool doctors and nurses use to measure how severe withdrawal is and to decide how much medication a person needs during detox.

What it measures

Ten symptoms: nausea and vomiting, tremor, sweating, anxiety, agitation, unusual skin sensations, unusual sounds, unusual sights, headache, and confusion about time and place.

How it is scored

Nine items are scored 0 to 7 and orientation 0 to 4, for a maximum of 67. Fewer than 8 points is usually minimal withdrawal, 8 to 15 moderate, and more than 15 severe.

How it is used

During medically supervised detox, staff repeat the CIWA-Ar every few hours. Rising scores signal a need for more medication or a higher level of care before complications develop.

Please read first

The CIWA-Ar is designed to be completed by a nurse or doctor, who observes things like tremor and sweating directly. This version uses plain language so that a person in withdrawal, or a family member sitting with them, can get a rough idea of severity. A family member’s answers are usually more accurate. A low score does not mean withdrawal is safe, because symptoms can worsen over the next two to three days. Your answers stay on your device; nothing is saved or sent.

The check

Answer for the last hour. If you can, have someone who is with you answer it about you.

Please read first. This self-check is for information only. It is not a diagnosis or medical advice, and it does not replace an assessment by a doctor or clinician. If you or the person you are with has any danger signs, get medical help now instead of finishing it. Read our medical disclaimer.

Who is answering?

Step 1: Danger signs

Tick any that apply right now or during this episode of withdrawal.

Danger signs

Get medical help now. Call 999 or 112, or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. Do not wait to finish this check. If you are with someone in withdrawal, stay with them.

Once they are safe, call us on +254 795 180 704. We are open 24 hours a day.

Step 2: Symptoms in the last hour

Choose the description closest to how things are right now.

Nausea and vomiting

Do you feel sick to your stomach? Have you vomited?

Tremor

Stretch both arms out in front with fingers spread. Look at the hands.

Sweating

Look at the forehead, palms and clothes.

Anxiety

Do you feel nervous, on edge, or afraid?

Restlessness (agitation)

Look at how much the person is moving.

Skin sensations

Any itching, pins and needles, burning, numbness, or a feeling of insects crawling on or under the skin?

Sounds

Are sounds harsher or more frightening than usual? Hearing anything that disturbs you, or things you know are not there?

Sights

Does light seem too bright or colours different? Seeing anything disturbing, or things you know are not there?

Headache or fullness in the head

Does the head feel different, tight like a band around it? (Do not count dizziness.)

Clear thinking (orientation)

What is today’s date? Where are you? Who am I? Can you add 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 in your head?
