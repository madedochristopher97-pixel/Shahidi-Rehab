The Addiction Severity Index: looking at the whole person

The ASI looks beyond the drinking or drug use itself, at seven areas of a person’s life: medical, employment and support, alcohol, drugs, legal, family and social, and psychiatric.

How it is done

A trained clinician carries out a structured interview of about 45 to 60 minutes, covering both the past 30 days and the person’s lifetime history.

How it is scored

The clinician gives each area a severity rating from 0 (no real problem) to 9 (extreme problem, treatment absolutely needed). Composite scores are also calculated to track change over time.

Why it matters

It shows where help is most needed, so a treatment plan can address work, family, health and mental wellbeing, not just the substance. Repeating it later shows whether recovery is taking hold.

Please read first

The full ASI can only be scored by a trained interviewer, so this is not an ASI score. This self-check uses the two questions the ASI asks you to answer yourself in each area: how troubled you have been in the past 30 days, and how much you want help with it. It gives you a picture of where addiction is touching your life. Your answers stay on your device; nothing is saved or sent.
